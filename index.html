Where the numbers come from

The calculators use no outside data. Every result is arithmetic on the balances, rates and payments you enter, with interest charged monthly at the APR ÷ 12. The guides cite named public sources, each linked and dated where it is used: the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau’s consumer answers, the Federal Trade Commission’s consumer advice on debt relief, the Federal Reserve’s G.19 Consumer Credit release for average bank rates, and peer-reviewed research such as the Journal of Marketing Research and Journal of Consumer Research studies on repayment behavior. Rate figures are checked against each new G.19 release, and cited agency pages are re-read when a guide is updated. The site is written and maintained by Debt Payoff Math. Every formula is set out on the methodology page.

Common questions

What is the monthly payment on a $200,000 loan at 6% for 30 years?

The principal and interest payment is $1,199.10 a month. Over 360 payments you would pay $231,676.38 in interest on top of the $200,000 borrowed. Taxes, insurance and any mortgage insurance are extra. More in Amortization Schedule Calculator.

Does paying extra principal lower my monthly payment?

Usually not; on a standard fixed-rate loan the required payment stays the same and the loan simply ends sooner. In the example above, $100 extra a month ends a 30-year loan 65 months early. More in Amortization Schedule Calculator.

Is a 15-year or 30-year schedule cheaper?

At the same rate, the 15-year schedule costs far less interest. At 6% on $200,000, 180 months means $1,687.71 a month and $103,788.46 of interest, against $1,199.10 and $231,676.38 over 360 months. More in Amortization Schedule Calculator.

What is the best credit card payoff strategy?

There is no single best strategy, but every plan works better once you stop adding new charges and pay more than the minimum. More in Credit Card Payoff Calculator.

How can I calculate my credit card payoff?

Enter your balance, the APR from your statement and your monthly payment into the calculator above. More in Credit Card Payoff Calculator.

How to pay off $10,000 credit card debt?

Pick a monthly payment well above the interest and hold it steady. More in Credit Card Payoff Calculator.
