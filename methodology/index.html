Methodology

Every tool on this site runs entirely in your browser and uses no external data. Results come only from the numbers you enter. This page explains each calculation. All results are estimates, rounded to the cent for display.

How interest is charged

All debt tools charge interest once a month at the APR ÷ 12 on the balance left after the previous payment. Each month the tool adds that interest, subtracts your payment (never more than the balance plus interest), and repeats until the balance reaches zero. If a payment does not cover the month’s interest, the tool reports that the debt will never be paid off. Runs are capped at 1,200 months.

Credit cards work differently. The CFPB explains (reviewed September 23, 2024) that issuers generally use a daily periodic rate, the APR divided by 360 or 365, applied to the balance each day. Because this site charges interest monthly, card results are close estimates and your statement will differ slightly. The tools also assume no new purchases.

Loan payment, amount and term

The fixed monthly payment is P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where P is the amount borrowed, r is the APR ÷ 1,200 and n is the number of months. At a 0% rate the payment is P ÷ n. The amount you can borrow for a given payment reverses the formula: payment × (1 − (1 + r)^−n) ÷ r. Payoff time is found by running the monthly cycle above. Total interest is the sum of the payments minus the amount borrowed.

Amortization schedule

The tool computes the fixed payment, adds any extra principal you enter, runs the monthly cycle, and groups every 12 months into one row showing principal paid, interest paid and the ending balance. Taxes, insurance and fees are not included.

Credit card payoff

Three modes: a fixed payment run through the monthly cycle; a deadline, which uses the loan payment formula for the number of months you choose; and minimum payments only, where each month’s payment is the larger of two figures: your card’s floor amount, or the percentage of the balance you enter (plus that month’s interest if you tick the box). Minimum-payment formulas differ by issuer, so you enter your own from your card agreement.

Debt snowball and debt avalanche

The monthly budget is the sum of all your minimum payments plus the extra you enter, and it stays fixed until every debt is gone. Each month the tool:

  1. adds interest at APR ÷ 12 to every open debt;
  2. pays each open debt its minimum, or its full balance if that is smaller;
  3. sends everything left in the budget to the target debt, and any remainder to the next debt in line.

The snowball orders debts from smallest balance to largest; the avalanche from highest APR to lowest. Ties keep the order you listed. When a debt is paid off, its minimum stays in the budget, so it rolls into the next target. Each tool also runs the other method on the same debts, and a minimums-only comparison that pays each debt its own minimum with nothing rolled over.

Debt consolidation

Your current plan runs each debt separately at the payment you enter, and adds up the interest; the longest payoff sets the current debt-free date. Because the origination fee is taken out of the new loan, the loan principal is total debt ÷ (1 − fee), so the full payoff amount reaches your creditors. The new payment uses the loan payment formula on that principal. The new loan’s cost is payment × term minus the debt paid off, which counts both interest and the fee. The saving is the current plan’s interest minus that cost; a negative saving is shown as extra cost.

Personal loan and true APR

The payment is calculated on the full loan amount at the stated interest rate. The money you receive is the amount × (1 − fee). The APR including the fee is the annual rate at which the present value of your fixed payments equals the cash you receive. The tool finds it by bisection between 0% and 100%, halving the range 200 times. With no fee, the APR equals the interest rate. The CFPB (reviewed August 28, 2026) describes the APR as the interest rate plus fees charged by the lender, including origination charges.

Interest calculator

Simple interest: ending balance = P × (1 + r × t). Compound interest: P × (1 + r ÷ k)^(k × t), with k = 1 for yearly, 12 for monthly and 365 for daily compounding, r the annual rate as a decimal and t the years. The effective annual rate (APY) is (1 + r ÷ k)^k − 1; for simple interest the tool shows the stated rate. Regulation DD, Appendix A sets out the federal APY formula for deposit accounts.

Debt payoff calculator

The tool runs your balance at your current payment, then runs the balance minus any one-time payment at your payment plus the monthly extra, and reports the months and interest saved. The payment to finish by a target month uses the loan payment formula on the full balance, ignoring the one-time and extra payments.

Debt-to-income ratio

DTI = total monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income × 100. The housing-only ratio uses the rent or mortgage payment alone. Everyday bills such as utilities and groceries are not counted. Lenders set their own limits.

Testing and updates

Every tool is checked against worked examples with exact expected results, for example $25,000 at 8% for 60 months gives a $506.91 payment, and $10,000 at 12% for 36 months with a 5% fee gives $332.14 a month and a 15.61% APR. Guide figures that come from outside sources, such as the Federal Reserve’s G.19 average rates, are checked against each new release and dated where they appear.
