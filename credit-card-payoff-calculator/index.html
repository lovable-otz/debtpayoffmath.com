How long will it take to pay off my credit card?

Payoff time depends on three numbers: the balance, the APR and what you pay each month. Your payment has to be larger than the month’s interest, or the balance never shrinks. Every dollar above that interest goes to principal, so small increases in the payment cut months off the end.

The tool works three ways: Pay a fixed amount, Be debt-free by a deadline, or See minimum payments only. The table below runs a $10,000 balance at 22.15%, the average rate on accounts assessed interest in the Federal Reserve’s G.19 release of September 8, 2026 (second quarter of 2026). It assumes no new purchases.

Plan for $10,000 at 22.15%Time to pay offTotal interest
$300 a month53 months$5,668.57
$382.68 a month36 months$3,776.51
$500 a month26 months$2,595.36
Minimum only (1% + interest, $35 floor)266 months$16,959.68

Going from $300 to $500 a month halves the time and saves $3,073.21. On minimums alone, payoff takes over 22 years and the interest exceeds the original balance.

Why minimum payments take so long

Minimum payments take so long because most card minimums shrink as the balance shrinks. A common formula is 1% of the balance plus that month’s interest, with a dollar floor. Early on, most of the payment is interest, and as the balance falls the payment falls with it, so principal comes down slowly for years.

Take a $5,000 balance at 22.99%. The first month’s interest is $95.79. Under a 1%-plus-interest formula the minimum is $145.79, and only $50 of it reduces the balance. Keep paying just the minimum, with a $35 floor, and the card takes 199 months to clear and costs $8,052.95 in interest. That is more than four times the $1,871.08 you would pay at a flat $200.

Your statement already warns you about this. Under Regulation Z § 1026.7(b)(12), issuers must print a minimum payment warning, an estimate of how long minimum payments will take, the total cost, and usually the monthly payment that would clear the balance in 36 months, plus a toll-free number for credit counseling. The CFPB explains (reviewed January 22, 2024) that you are not required to pay the 36-month amount, and that the figures assume no new purchases.

Minimum formulas vary by issuer, so enter the percentage and floor from your card agreement.

How is credit card interest charged?

Most card issuers charge interest daily. The CFPB (reviewed January 22, 2024) says many companies apply a daily periodic rate to your average daily balance. The daily periodic rate is generally your APR divided by 360 or 365, depending on the issuer.

The issuer averages each day’s balance over the billing cycle and applies the daily rate for every day in the cycle, so a payment made earlier in the cycle trims a little interest.

Two more rules from the CFPB page shape your bill:

  • Grace period. You usually avoid interest on new purchases only if you pay the full balance by the due date. Once you carry a balance, new purchases may be charged interest right away.
  • Payment order. When you pay more than the minimum, the extra generally goes to the balance with the highest APR first.

This calculator charges interest once a month at APR ÷ 12, so its figures are close estimates. Your statement will differ by a few dollars depending on your issuer’s method and when you pay.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best credit card payoff strategy?

There is no single best strategy, but every plan works better once you stop adding new charges and pay more than the minimum. With several cards, the avalanche method (highest APR first) usually costs the least interest, while the snowball method (smallest balance first) clears accounts sooner. Try your own cards in the avalanche and snowball calculators and compare the totals.

How can I calculate my credit card payoff?

Enter your balance, the APR from your statement and your monthly payment into the calculator above. Behind the scenes, each month it adds interest (balance × APR ÷ 12), subtracts your payment, and repeats until the balance reaches zero. Your statement also shows the issuer’s own minimum-payment payoff estimate and, in most cases, the payment that clears the balance in 36 months.

How to pay off $10,000 credit card debt?

Pick a monthly payment well above the interest and hold it steady. At 22.15%, the Q2 2026 average for cards charging interest (Federal Reserve G.19), $10,000 takes 53 months and $5,668.57 in interest at $300 a month, or 26 months and $2,595.36 at $500. Clearing it in 36 months takes $382.68 a month. These estimates assume no new purchases.

How to pay off $30,000 in credit card debt quickly?

Speed comes from the size of the payment. At 22.15%, $30,000 takes 45 months and $14,116.51 in interest at $1,000 a month, or 26 months and $7,786.07 at $1,500. Paying it off in one year would take $2,810.00 a month. If the payments are out of reach, the CFPB describes nonprofit credit counselors who can set up a debt management plan.
