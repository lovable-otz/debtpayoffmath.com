Debt Avalanche Calculator

A debt avalanche calculator ranks your debts by APR and puts every spare dollar on the highest rate first. With $9,500 across two cards and a car loan plus $200 extra a month, the avalanche finishes in 27 months with $1,115.15 of interest, $56.51 less than the snowball order.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Debt Payoff Math

What is the debt avalanche method?

The debt avalanche is a payoff order that targets the most expensive debt first. You pay the minimum on everything, then send all extra money to the debt with the highest APR. When it is paid off, its payment rolls to the next-highest rate. Because costly balances shrink first, it keeps total interest as low as any fixed order can.

With the default debts, the order is Card B (24%), then Card A (18%), then the car loan (7%). Your budget stays at $405 a month the whole way:

Card issuers already use this logic inside a single card. The CFPB (reviewed January 22, 2024) notes that payments above the minimum generally go to the highest-APR balance first. The avalanche applies the same rule across all your accounts.

How much does the avalanche save?

On the default debts, the avalanche saves $56.51 compared with the snowball and $3,467.07 compared with paying only the minimums. Both methods finish in month 27. The difference comes only from which debt gets the extra $200 first. Interest here is estimated monthly at APR ÷ 12 with no new borrowing.

The saving over the snowball grows with the spread between your rates and the size of the high-rate balances. In the Federal Reserve G.19 release of September 8, 2026, the average rate on credit cards charging interest was 22.15% for the second quarter of 2026, while 24-month personal loans at commercial banks averaged 11.86%. A household with both kinds of debt has a wide spread to work with.

What if two debts have the same rate?

When two debts share a rate, the order between them does not change your total interest; every dollar of principal you pay removes the same interest either way. The order only changes when an account closes. Paying the smaller one first frees its minimum sooner, which can simplify your budget.

Here is a check using the same formulas as this tool: a $3,000 debt and a $1,500 debt, both at 20%, with minimums of $75 and $40 and $150 extra a month.

The totals match to the cent, but the smaller-first order closes an account seven months earlier. For a tie, the calculator keeps the order you entered, so consider listing the smaller balance first.

Rates can also change mid-plan. The CFPB (reviewed September 23, 2024) says an introductory balance transfer rate must last at least six months unless you are more than 60 days late, and the card must disclose the rate that follows. Frequently asked questions

Does Dave Ramsey recommend snowball or avalanche?

Ramsey Solutions recommends the snowball. Its comparison page (updated January 22, 2025) says starting with the highest rate may make sense mathematically, but that the quick wins from clearing the smallest debt first provide the motivation people need to finish. The avalanche puts interest cost first; the snowball puts early momentum first.

How many Americans are 100% debt free?

About 23% of U.S. families held no debt at all in 2022. The Federal Reserve’s Survey of Consumer Finances (published October 2023) found that 77.4% of families had some kind of debt, including mortgages, car loans, student loans and credit cards. The survey measures families, not individual adults.

How fast can I pay off $20,000 in debt?

At a 22.15% card rate, $20,000 takes 26 months at $1,000 a month, with $5,190.71 in interest, or 74 months at $500, with $16,651.61. A 12-month finish needs $1,873.33 a month. If the $20,000 is split across debts with different rates, enter them above: the avalanche shows the fastest-saving order for your payment.
