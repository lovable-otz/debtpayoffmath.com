How are personal loan payments calculated?

Personal loan payments are calculated with the standard amortization formula: the loan amount, the monthly rate (the annual rate ÷ 12) and the number of months set one fixed payment that repays the balance exactly on schedule. The payment is based on the full amount borrowed, even when a fee is taken out first.

Every payment covers that month’s interest first, and the rest reduces the balance. At 12% the monthly rate is 1%, so the first month’s interest on $10,000 is $100.00, and $232.14 of the $332.14 payment goes to principal.

Term matters as much as rate. At 11.86%, the average bank rate on 24-month personal loans in the second quarter of 2026 according to the Federal Reserve G.19 release of September 8, 2026, a $10,000 loan costs:

TermMonthly paymentTotal interest
24 months$470.08$1,281.95
36 months$331.47$1,933.09
60 months$221.74$3,304.26

These use the Fed’s 24-month average for every term, only to show the effect of length; a lender may price longer terms differently. For a loan without a fee, the loan calculator also works backward from a payment you can afford.

What is an origination fee?

An origination fee is what a lender charges for making the loan. This tool treats it as a percentage taken out of the loan before you receive it, while you still repay the full amount with interest. The CFPB lists it (reviewed August 30, 2024) among the common personal installment loan fees, alongside documentation fees and credit insurance.

Because the payment is set on the full balance, a fee raises your real cost without changing the payment. Using the tool’s default loan, $10,000 at 12% for 36 months:

Origination feeCash you receiveMonthly paymentAPR including the fee
0%$10,000$332.1412.00%
1%$9,900$332.1412.70%
3%$9,700$332.1414.13%
5%$9,500$332.1415.61%
8%$9,200$332.1417.90%

If you need the full $10,000 in hand, divide by one minus the fee. With a 5% fee that means borrowing $10,526.32, which raises the payment to $349.62. The debt consolidation calculator builds this gross-up in automatically.

Interest rate vs APR

The interest rate is the price of borrowing the principal. The APR adds the lender’s upfront fees. The CFPB (reviewed August 28, 2026) defines the APR as the interest rate “plus any additional fees charged by the lender,” including origination charges. With no fee, the two match.

The tool finds the APR by working out which yearly rate makes your 36 fixed payments exactly equal the cash you receive. For the $10,000 loan with a 5% fee, payments of $332.14 against $9,500 received give 15.61%, and the total cost of borrowing is $2,457.15 instead of $1,957.15 without the fee.

Term changes the fee’s effect. The same 5% fee adds more to the APR on a short loan, because it is spread over fewer months: 17.23% over 24 months and 14.28% over 60 months at a 12% rate.

Lenders must disclose the APR under the Truth in Lending Act before you sign, and the CFPB suggests comparing “APRs to APRs.” That figure is the fairest way to line up two offers.

Frequently asked questions

How much would a $30,000 personal loan cost per month?

About $1,410.24 a month over 24 months or $665.21 over 60 months, assuming 11.86%, the Federal Reserve’s average bank rate for 24-month loans in the second quarter of 2026. Total interest would be $3,845.85 or $9,912.78. Your own rate depends on the lender and your credit; enter an actual quote in the tool.

How much will a $20,000 loan cost a month?

At 11.86%, a $20,000 loan costs $940.16 a month over 24 months, $662.95 over 36 months or $443.48 over 60 months. The 60-month option costs $6,608.52 in interest, more than two and a half times the $2,563.90 on the 24-month loan. An origination fee does not change these payments but does raise the APR.

How much would a $10,000 personal loan cost per month?

Roughly $331 to $470 a month for a 36- or 24-month term at 11.86%, and $221.74 over 60 months. At the tool’s default 12% for 36 months the payment is $332.14. If a 5% fee is deducted, you receive $9,500 but still repay the full $10,000 plus interest.

How much would a $15,000 loan cost?

At 11.86%, a $15,000 loan costs $1,922.92 in interest over 24 months ($705.12 a month) or $4,956.39 over 60 months ($332.61 a month). Add any origination fee to find the full cost. These figures assume the Fed’s 24-month average rate; a real quote may be higher or lower.
